Why can understanding yourself leave your life unchanged?

Is explaining a predicament part of getting out of it, or another way of staying where you are?

My current view

I tend to distinguish understanding a problem from changing it. Recognising its causes may change the actions a person considers, but time, resources, physical demands and the conditions needed to act may remain unchanged.

On the other hand, new understanding can directly reduce shame, fear or mistaken expectations. A path that was always feasible but never considered may suddenly become part of someone's life. In such cases, understanding itself may be an effective intervention.

What remains unclear

I have not yet drawn a sufficiently clear boundary between the two. Which difficulties can substantially improve through a new understanding, and which require practical conditions to change first? Can excessive analysis eventually make taking action harder?

What would change my mind

One approach is to compare actions before and after a new explanation, rather than comparing only immediate emotions. If actions keep improving without additional resources or support, cognitive change deserves greater explanatory weight. If analysis becomes more frequent without more effective action, that is worth documenting too.
