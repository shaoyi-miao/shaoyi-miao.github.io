Habits · Ability · Open discussion

When tasks become easier, can people lose the ability to do them independently?

Tools, automation and outsourcing reduce everyday costs. But where does the effort they save actually go?

My current view

I tend to think that reducing the friction of getting started can make certain actions more likely, but saving effort and developing ability are different things. Reducing repetitive work may free up time, yet it may also remove the practice needed to maintain a skill.

The key may not be whether a tool is used, but whether it replaces mechanical execution or judgment, practice and error correction — and whether the time saved actually goes into more valuable activities.

What remains unclear

I do not yet have a clear boundary for deciding when a tool extends someone's ability and when it merely transfers an ability that once belonged to that person to an external system. Might the answer be entirely different across tasks?

What would change my mind

If long-term tool users also improve their independent performance after the tool is removed, or if a tool largely replaces practice without reducing ability, a simple deterioration hypothesis needs revision. I would welcome discussion of specific tasks, amounts of practice, quality of performance and results after removing the tool, rather than relying solely on users' impressions.
