How much can a person's principles cost before they give way?

When someone's behaviour changes under pressure, how can I distinguish a change in values from a change in practical circumstances?

My current view

I tend to think that many apparently sudden changes in character should first prompt an examination of whether the cost of maintaining the previous behaviour has changed. At the same time, genuine commitments, responsibilities and priorities among values can influence action before it occurs.

Sticking to a principle cannot therefore simply be explained by low costs, nor can every compromise be taken as evidence that the person never had principles. These two explanations need distinct evidence from actual behaviour.

What remains unclear

The difficulty is observing relatively stable weights assigned to values without retrospectively explaining every action through some unobservable hidden cost. There may be no universal threshold across different people, responsibilities and pressures.

What would change my mind

If behaviour remains stable over time despite a substantial change in costs under comparable conditions, commitments, identity and norms deserve serious consideration. If the original behaviour returns when costs fall, changing costs may have greater explanatory power. What specific observations could distinguish these mechanisms more clearly?
