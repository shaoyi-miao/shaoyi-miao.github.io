Relationships · Freedom · Open discussion

Are people more willing to stay when they are free to leave?

Is commitment in a relationship stable because leaving is difficult, or more meaningful because leaving is possible?

My current view

I tend to think that a genuine right to say no, and the ability to bear the cost of leaving, make long-term commitment more likely to remain a continuing choice rather than merely the result of existing ties.

That does not mean greater independence always makes a relationship better. Sharing a life naturally involves a division of responsibilities, obligations and dependence. Constantly preserving an exit option may also reduce investment and trust.

What remains unclear

I do not yet know at which stages of a relationship, or under which shared responsibilities and external conditions, a greater ability to leave improves cooperation, and when it weakens long-term investment by both partners. It is especially difficult to distinguish the effect of exit options from those of income, health and pre-existing trust.

What would change my mind

If making exit options more accessible leads to less renegotiation and less stable cooperation in otherwise comparable relationships, or if relationships with few exit options but genuine protections perform better, I would need to narrow my original judgment. Experiences or research that distinguish these factors are welcome.
